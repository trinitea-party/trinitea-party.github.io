514th Fighter Squadron "Phượng Hoàng"


23rd Tactical Wing - Biên Hòa Air Base

Originally, the 514th Fighter Squadron was called 1st Fighter Squadron, as it was the very first air unit to be activated for the Republic of Vietnam. The squadron was nicknamed "Phượng Hoàng", which means "Phoenix". The unit first flew F8F Bearcats and L-19 Bird Dog planes. In 1960, they began receiving A-1H from America after some of their pilots completed a training program in America. In 1963, the army reorganized, and the 1st Fighter squadron was renamed to the 514th Fighter Squadron. The A-1 was grounded in early 1974 due to a lack of spare parts for the aging fleet, but the 514th FS resumed operations in early April 1975, as the ARVN needed everything it had to be doing something useful. The squadron fought until noon on April 30th, 1975, until it was decided to evacuate.

During the evacuation, Lieutenant Colonel Hồ Văn Hiển crammed 25 people into the rear blue cabin of an A-1E, and flew to U-Tapao Air Base, Thailand. His A-1E aircraft is now on display at the National Museum of World War II Aviation in Colorado Springs, Colorado (currently it is painted to look like a Skyraider of 524th Squadron).
